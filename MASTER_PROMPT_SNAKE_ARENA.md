## 2. CORE LOOP AND DESIGN PILLARS

### 2.1 The loop

```
SPAWN small
  → MOVE (continuous steering)
    → COLLECT power cells / shards        (dopamine every ~0.5s)
      → GROW length + POWER value          (visible progress every ~3s)
        → THREAT ASSESSMENT (who is bigger/smaller than me?)
          → OUTMANOEUVRE: cut off a smaller rival, flee a larger one
            → ABSORB their dropped mass    (big payoff spike every ~20-40s)
              → CLIMB the leaderboard
                → DIE (eventually) → SCORE SCREEN → REWARDED REVIVE or PLAY AGAIN
                  → loop
```

### 2.2 Design pillars (use these to settle any argument)

1. **Readable at a glance.** A new player understands who they can eat within 2 seconds of looking at the screen. Colour + size + a floating Power number, all three redundantly.
2. **Momentum, not precision.** The game is about arcs and cut-offs, not pixel-perfect input. Turning has a turn-rate limit; it should feel like steering a boat, not a cursor.
3. **Growth is the reward.** Every single collect must produce visible, audible, tactile feedback. Growth is never silent.
4. **Death is fast and fair.** You always understand why you died. Death-to-next-run must be ≤ 6 seconds if the player skips the ad-adjacent options.
5. **Respect the player's time and attention.** Ads are frequent enough to be a business and rare enough to not be hated. Rewarded > interstitial always.

### 2.3 Explicit anti-goals

* No grid-locked stepping movement.
* No pay-to-win. Ever. See `§45.9`.
* No dark patterns: no fake close buttons, no unskippable ads inside gameplay, no forced ads before the *first* run of a session.
* No procedural over-engineering (no ECS rewrite, no custom physics engine, no networking) before the core loop is fun.

### 3.4 Boost

* Costs `boost_power_drain = 2.2` power/second while held.
* Cannot boost below `min_boost_power = 4.0`.
* Drained power is **emitted behind you as `corpse_mote` collectibles at 4/second** — this is the classic risk/reward loop and it feeds the arena economy.
* No cooldown; the *cost* is the limiter. (This replaces the original cooldown-bar design — logged in `§46`. A cooldown-based variant must remain switchable via `boost_mode: enum {DRAIN, COOLDOWN}` in the config resource.)
* HUD shows a power-drain ring around the boost button rather than a cooldown bar.

### 3.5 Arena

* Shape: **circle**, radius `120.0` units. Circular avoids corner camping and makes the minimap trivial.
* Boundary: **Option A+B hybrid** — a visible translucent energy wall with a hex-pattern shader, plus a *soft* zone: the outer `8.0` units tint red, slow you `0.85×`, and push you inward. Touching the actual wall does **not** kill you (that feels unfair); it hard-stops your outward velocity and you slide along it. AI treats the soft zone as a hazard.
* Log this decision: chosen because instant-death walls punish new players and wrapping worlds destroy the minimap's readability.

### 3.6 Session flow / difficulty curve

* `0–20s`: no AI within 35 units of spawn; only `cell_small`/`cell_medium` nearby. Free growth. This is the tutorial-by-doing window.
* `20–60s`: AI density ramps to full; first `shard_rare` spawns.
* `60s+`: "Surge" every 45 s — a cluster of 40 collectibles and 1 rare shard spawns at a random point, announced by a beam of light and a HUD ping. This creates deliberate conflict hotspots.
* Soft session pressure: after `180 s` the arena radius shrinks by `0.6 units/s` down to a floor of `70.0`, forcing endgame confrontation. Announce it clearly ("ARENA COLLAPSING").

## 5. CAMERA

* `Camera3D` under a `CameraRig` (Node3D) — never move the Camera3D directly.
* Angled top-down: pitch `-58°` at start, easing toward `-68°` as the player grows (higher power = more top-down = more situational awareness).
* Distance: `dist = 17.0 + 11.0 * pow(power_value, 0.30)`, clamped `[17, 62]`.
* Follow: critically-damped spring (`smoothing_half_life = 0.12 s`), **not** `lerp(x, y, delta)` — frame-rate-independent. Provide `Smoothing.damp(current, target, half_life, delta)` in `scripts/util/smoothing.gd` and use it everywhere.
* **Look-ahead:** camera target = `player_head + velocity_dir * lookahead_distance` where `lookahead_distance = 3.5 + speed * 0.22`. Reduce look-ahead to 40% while boosting so the player doesn't lose their own head off-centre.
* **Camera shake:** trauma-based (Squirrel Eiserloh model). `add_trauma(amount)`; shake = `trauma²`; decays at `1.4/s`. Triggers: eat a snake `0.55`, die `0.9`, hit wall `0.15`, rare shard `0.3`. **Must be reducible to 0 via a Settings toggle (accessibility).**
* **Zoom must never cause the head to be occluded.** Materials on the boundary wall use `depth_draw_opaque` off and fade near camera.
* Support a `CameraProfile` resource so styles (close/arcade, far/tactical, cinematic) can be swapped. Default profile selectable in Settings.
* Mobile: FOV and distance get a `1.12×` multiplier because screens are smaller and held closer.

## 6. MOVEMENT AND BODY-FOLLOWING (THE MOST IMPORTANT TECHNICAL SECTION)

### 6.1 Head movement

* Head is a `CharacterBody3D` (or plain `Node3D` moved manually — decide and log it; manual is faster, but `CharacterBody3D` gives free wall sliding. **Recommendation: plain `Node3D` + manual movement + a single `Area3D` for detection**, because we do our own collision (`§6.4`)).
* Movement is on the **XZ plane only**. Y is fixed at `0`. Do not allow drift.
* Steering: the head has a `facing_angle: float`. Each physics tick, compute `desired_angle` from input, then rotate `facing_angle` toward it by at most `turn_rate * delta`. Never snap.
* **Everything gameplay-critical runs in `_physics_process` at 60 Hz.** Set `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second = 60` and enable `physics_interpolation` (Godot 4.3+) OR do your own render-side interpolation. Non-negotiable for web builds where frame rate is erratic.

### 6.2 Body following — chosen algorithm

**Use a distance-sampled position history ring buffer.** This is the decided approach; do not substitute another without logging a deviation.

```
Every physics tick:
  if head.position.distance_to(history[head_index]) >= history_sample_distance:
      push head.position (+ facing) into ring buffer

Every physics tick, for each segment i:
  target_arc_distance = (i + 1) * segment_spacing
  sample the ring buffer at that arc-length (walking back, LERPING between the two
  bracketing samples — never snapping to the nearest sample)
  segment.global_position = interpolated point
  segment.look_at(previous_segment_position)
```

Why: O(n) per frame with a tiny constant, perfectly smooth, trivially supports growth/shrink (just change how deep you read), and does not need physics bodies.

**Critical implementation details you must get right:**
* Preallocate the ring buffer (`PackedVector3Array` of `max_history_points`). **Never** `append`/`pop_front` on an Array in the hot loop — that allocates and causes GC hitches.
* Cache cumulative arc-length alongside each sample so segment lookup is a backward walk from a cached index, not a re-scan from zero. Each segment starts its search from the previous segment's found index → total cost is O(history_span), not O(n·history).
* When the snake grows, new segments spawn **at the position of the current last segment**, then ease into place over `0.25 s`. Never pop into existence at the origin.
* When the snake shrinks, segments play a 0.2 s scale-down then return to the pool.
* Segments are `MultiMeshInstance3D`-backed where possible (`§36.2`) — one MultiMesh per snake, one instance per segment. This is the single biggest perf win. Fall back to individual `MeshInstance3D` only if MultiMesh proves problematic, and log it.

### 6.3 Turn-radius exploit guard

A snake must not be able to turn tighter than its own body width and self-intersect trivially. Enforce `min_turn_radius = current_radius * 1.15` by clamping `turn_rate` accordingly. Self-collision is **disabled** by default (`self_collision_enabled = false` in config) because in a continuous game it feels arbitrary; expose it as a toggle for a future "Hardcore" mode.

### 6.4 Collision — do NOT use physics bodies for bodies

* Head: one `Area3D` sphere per snake (player + AI). That's it.
* Bodies: **spatial-hash broadphase you write yourself** in `SpatialHash.gd` — a `Dictionary` keyed by `Vector2i(floor(x/cell), floor(z/cell))` with `cell_size = 6.0`. Each snake registers its segment positions once per physics tick (or every 2nd tick for distant AI, see `§36`).
* Head-vs-body test: query the hash for the head's cell + 8 neighbours, then do squared-distance checks against segment positions. Sum total per frame with 9 snakes ≈ a few hundred float comparisons. Cheap.
* Collectibles: also in the spatial hash. Collection is a radius query, **not** an Area3D per collectible (400 Area3Ds will destroy the web build).

**This is the performance backbone of the entire game. Build `SpatialHash.gd` in Phase 1 and write a unit test for it (`§9A`).**

## 7. INPUT ARCHITECTURE

Create `InputManager` (autoload) exposing exactly:

```gdscript
signal boost_pressed
signal boost_released
func get_steer_direction(from_world_pos: Vector3) -> Vector3  # normalized XZ, or ZERO
func is_boosting() -> bool
func get_active_scheme() -> Scheme  # MOUSE, KEYBOARD, TOUCH, GAMEPAD
```

Gameplay code **must never** call `Input.*` directly. Zero exceptions.

* **Desktop mouse (default):** raycast from camera through mouse onto the Y=0 plane; steer toward that point. Add a `dead_zone_radius = 1.2` units around the head so tiny jitter doesn't wobble the snake. Boost = Left Mouse Button held **or** Space.
* **Desktop keyboard:** WASD / arrows give an absolute direction vector; the turn-rate limiter handles the rest. Boost = Shift or Space.
* **Touch:** dynamic virtual joystick — on touch-down anywhere in the left 65% of the screen, place an invisible origin; drag gives direction. `joystick_max_radius = 90 px`, dead zone `12 px`. Boost = a thumb button bottom-right (with a `1.35×` invisible touch-target margin) **or** double-tap-and-hold anywhere.
* **Gamepad:** left stick steers, `A`/cross boosts. (Cheap to add, meaningfully widens the audience.)
* **Auto-detect and switch schemes at runtime** on first input of a new type; never require a settings change.
* Godot **Input Map actions** (define all in `project.godot`): `steer_up`, `steer_down`, `steer_left`, `steer_right`, `boost`, `pause`, `debug_toggle`, `ui_confirm`, `ui_cancel`.
* **Input must be centrally suspendable:** `InputManager.set_suspended(true)` — used by pause, game-over, and critically by the ad layer (`§45.6`). When suspended, `get_steer_direction` returns the last held direction and `is_boosting()` returns `false`.

## 8. AI OPPONENTS — FULL SPECIFICATION

### 8.1 Architecture

`AIController` (owns decision-making) drives the same `SnakeController` the player uses, through the *same* interface (`set_steer_target(Vector3)`, `set_boost(bool)`). **If the AI can do something the player physically cannot, that is a bug.**

### 8.2 Two-layer design

**Layer 1 — Finite State Machine** (`AIStateMachine.gd`, states as separate scripts in `scripts/ai/states/`):

| State | Enter when | Behaviour | Exit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WANDER` | nothing interesting nearby | drift toward arena-centre-biased random waypoint, re-picked every 3–6 s | food or threat detected |
| `COLLECT` | collectibles within `sense_radius` | pathless steer to best-scoring cluster | threat detected / cluster consumed |
| `HUNT` | a snake with `power < mine / eat_power_ratio` within `aggro_radius` | intercept: aim at target's **predicted position**, and if `personality.cutoff_skill > rand()`, aim to cross *ahead* of them rather than chase the tail | target escapes, grows, or dies |
| `FLEE` | a snake with `power > mine * eat_power_ratio` within `fear_radius` | steer along the vector away from threat, blended with "toward open space" | threat > `fear_radius * 1.4` away |
| `AVOID_WALL` | inside soft boundary zone or predicted to be in `1.2 s` | highest priority steer, toward arena centre | clear |
| `AVOID_BODY` | raycast/probe ahead hits any snake body in `1.0 s` of travel | steer to the side with more open space | clear |
| `SCAVENGE` | a snake died within `40` units in the last `6 s` | rush the corpse motes | motes gone or timeout |
| `RECOVER` | just took a near-miss or dropped below 40% of peak power | play safe: only `COLLECT` at arena edge-ish quiet areas, no boosting, for `5 s` | timer |

**Priority order (a strict override chain, evaluated top-down):** `AVOID_WALL` > `AVOID_BODY` > `FLEE` > `HUNT` > `SCAVENGE` > `COLLECT` > `RECOVER` > `WANDER`.

**Layer 2 — Context steering** (this is what makes them feel alive, not robotic): each state produces a *desired direction*, then a shared **8-direction (or 16) context map** weights danger vs interest and picks the best heading. This yields smooth, natural evasion instead of jittery target-snapping. Implement in `ContextSteering.gd`.

### 8.3 Personalities (each a `.tres`)

| Personality | aggro_radius | fear_radius | greed | cutoff_skill | boost_willingness | reaction_delay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collector | 14 | 34 | 1.0 | 0.10 | 0.15 | 0.30 s |
| Aggressive | 42 | 18 | 0.4 | 0.55 | 0.75 | 0.18 s |
| Defensive | 8 | 46 | 0.7 | 0.05 | 0.40 | 0.22 s |
| Explorer | 20 | 30 | 0.6 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.28 s |
| Opportunist | 30 | 30 | 0.8 | 0.70 | 0.55 | 0.20 s |

### 8.4 Making AI feel fair and human

* **`reaction_delay`**: AI perceives a *stale* world snapshot, delayed by this amount. This single feature is what stops AI from feeling omniscient. Implement with a small ring buffer of world snapshots.
* **`aim_error`**: add `randf_range(-aim_error, aim_error)` degrees (start `6.0`, less for aggressive) to the chosen heading, resampled every `0.4 s` via a smoothed noise value (not per-frame random, which looks like a seizure).
* **Sense limits:** AI only knows about snakes within `sense_radius = 55` units and collectibles within `28`. No global omniscience.
* **Mistakes are mandatory:** each AI has a `blunder_chance` (`0.04`) evaluated every 2 s that makes it hold a bad heading for `0.5 s`. Players need to be able to win.
* **Rubber-banding is FORBIDDEN.** Do not scale AI skill by player score. Instead, control difficulty through the *mix* of personalities spawned and their starting power. Log any exception.
* **Names:** AI snakes get names from `resources/data/ai_names.txt` (you generate ~120 original, non-infringing, short handles like "Vex", "Nova", "Coil9", "Hexer"). Never reuse a name in one match.

### 8.5 AI performance budget

* Decision tick: `10 Hz`, **staggered** — each AI gets a random phase offset so they never all think on the same frame.
* Distant AI (> 90 units from the player, off-screen): decision tick drops to `3 Hz`, body-segment position updates drop to every 3rd physics tick, and their MultiMesh is culled. They must still play correctly (they can still eat and be eaten) — this is a level-of-detail optimization, not a simulation shortcut.
* Hard budget: **all AI combined must consume < 2.5 ms/frame** on the target hardware. Measure it and put the number in the final report.

## 9. COLLISION AND COMBAT RULES (MUST BE UNAMBIGUOUS)

| Event | Result |
|---|---|
| My head → their body, `my_power >= their_power * 1.10` | **They die. I absorb.** |
| My head → their body, otherwise | **I die.** (Running into a bigger snake's body kills you — this is the core tension.) |
| Head → head, power difference > 10% | Bigger eats smaller. |
| Head → head, within 10% | **Both die.** Rare, dramatic, fair. Big camera shake, both drop full mass. |
| Head → boundary wall | No death. Velocity clamped, slide along wall, `0.15` trauma, red vignette pulse. |
| Head → own body | Nothing (self-collision off by default). |
| Head → obstacle (if enabled) | Same as boundary: slide, no death. |
| Head → collectible | Absorb. |

**Readability requirement:** the moment before a lethal outcome, the threatening snake must already be visually flagged — bigger snakes render with a **red-tinted rim light** when they can eat you and a **green rim light** when you can eat them, at all times, updated every 4 physics ticks. This is the single most important UX feature in the game. Do not skip it.

On death (any snake): drop `dropped_mass_fraction = 0.72` of power as `corpse_mote` collectibles scattered along the body's last path (so the corpse visually traces where the snake was), each mote worth `power/mote_count`, with a `0.35 s` staggered spawn so it looks like a dissolving trail.

## 12. DEATH, SCORING, AND THE GAME-OVER FLOW

### 12.1 On player death (exact sequence, with timings)

1. `t=0.00` — Freeze input (`InputManager.set_suspended(true)`), `EventBus.player_died` emitted, camera trauma `0.9`, hit-stop: `Engine.time_scale = 0.15` for `0.18 s` real time, then ease back to `1.0` over `0.4 s`.
2. `t=0.18` — Body dissolves head-to-tail over `0.55 s` (per-segment scale-down + emissive flash), motes spawn along the path.
3. `t=0.30` — Fire the **analytics** event `run_ended` (`§45.10`).
4. `t=0.75` — Camera pulls back and up to frame the death site.
5. `t=1.10` — Game-over panel slides in.

### 12.2 Game-over panel (exact content)

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                RUN OVER                │
│                                        │
│   SCORE          12,450   (best 18,900)│
│   POWER            1,024               │
│   LENGTH             184               │

└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 12.3 Scoring

| Source | Score |
|---|---|
| Collectible | per `§3.3` table |
| Absorbing a rival | `250 + floor(their_power * 4)` |

## 19. PERFORMANCE BUDGET (HARD NUMBERS — MEASURE, DON'T GUESS)

Target: **60 FPS**, frame budget `16.6 ms`, allocated:

| System | Budget |
|---|---|
| Snake bodies (all 9) | 3.0 ms |
| AI decisions | 2.5 ms |
| Spatial hash rebuild + queries | 1.5 ms |
| Collectibles + spawning | 1.0 ms |
| Particles / VFX | 2.0 ms |
| UI | 1.5 ms |
| Rendering (draw calls) | 4.0 ms |
| Headroom | 1.1 ms |

Rules:
* **Max 150 draw calls.** Use MultiMesh aggressively: one MultiMesh for all `cell_small`, one for `cell_medium`, etc.; one per snake body.

# 45. MONETIZATION AND ADVERTISING ARCHITECTURE ★ CRITICAL SECTION ★

**This game earns money from advertising. The ad layer is a first-class system, designed in from Phase 1, not bolted on at the end.**

### 45.1 Architectural principle: total abstraction

Define one interface, `scripts/ads/ad_provider.gd`:

```gdscript
class_name AdProvider
extends RefCounted

# Lifecycle
func initialize(config: AdConfig, consent: ConsentState) -> void: pass
func is_initialized() -> bool: return false
func shutdown() -> void: pass

# Availability
func is_available(placement: AdPlacement) -> bool: return false
func preload_ad(placement: AdPlacement) -> void: pass

# Showing. All are async and MUST always resolve, even on error/timeout.
# Result codes: SHOWN_COMPLETED, SHOWN_SKIPPED, NO_FILL, ERROR, TIMEOUT, BLOCKED, DISABLED
func show_interstitial(placement: AdPlacement) -> AdResult: return AdResult.disabled()
func show_rewarded(placement: AdPlacement) -> AdResult: return AdResult.disabled()

# Banners
func show_banner(position: BannerPosition) -> void: pass
func hide_banner() -> void: pass
func get_banner_height_px() -> int: return 0

# Signals (emitted by AdManager, not by gameplay):
#   ad_started(placement), ad_finished(placement, result),
#   ad_reward_granted(placement, reward_id), ad_failed(placement, reason)
```

Concrete implementations:

| Provider | Purpose | Ships in v1 |
|---|---|---|
| `AdProviderNull` | Everything returns `DISABLED` immediately. Used on desktop dev builds and in automated tests. | ✅ |
| `AdProviderMock` | Simulates the full flow with a fake 5-second overlay, configurable fill rate, latency, and failure injection. **This is how you test ads without an SDK.** | ✅ |
| `AdProviderWebPortal` | JavaScriptBridge-based; targets Poki / CrazyGames / GameDistribution APIs. | ✅ (code + shell; needs human SDK keys) |
| `AdProviderAdMob` | Android/iOS via a Godot AdMob plugin. | ⚠️ Scaffolded — **cannot be completed by AI**, see `§47` |

`AdManager` (autoload) picks the provider at runtime:

```
if OS.has_feature("web") and portal_sdk_detected():  AdProviderWebPortal
elif OS.has_feature("mobile") and admob_plugin_present(): AdProviderAdMob
elif config.use_mock_in_editor and OS.has_feature("editor"): AdProviderMock
else: AdProviderNull
```

**The game must be fully playable and shippable with `AdProviderNull`.** Verify this explicitly and report it.

### 45.2 Concrete SDK notes (research these, they are current)

* **Mobile — AdMob.** The maintained option for Godot 4.2+ is the **Poing Studios AdMob plugin**, which supports App Open, Banner (incl. collapsible), Interstitial, Rewarded, Rewarded Interstitial, and Native, with GDScript/C# parity, in-editor mock ads, UMP (GDPR) consent, and impression-level revenue callbacks. There is also a **Cengiz AdMob plugin** with a node-based, Inspector-configurable API and a rich signal set. Pick one, install it under `addons/`, and wrap it behind `AdProviderAdMob` — no AdMob type may appear anywhere outside that one file. Always use Google's **official test ad unit IDs** during development (e.g. Android interstitial `ca-app-pub-3940256099942544/1033173712`); never ship with real IDs you invented, and never click your own live ads.
* **Web — Poki.** The Poki SDK model is: `gameLoadingFinished()` → `commercialBreak()` → `gameplayStart()`; on death `gameplayStop()` then either `commercialBreak()` (restart) or `rewardedBreak()` (revive). `rewardedBreak()` resolves with a boolean indicating whether the reward was actually earned. Poki also requires that you **mute audio and disable input for the duration of the break** — which is exactly what `§45.6` mandates. Wire these through `AdProviderWebPortal` using `JavaScriptBridge.get_interface("PokiSDK")` and a custom HTML shell.
* **Web — CrazyGames / GameDistribution / Y8** have equivalent "midgame" and "rewarded" break APIs. Structure `AdProviderWebPortal` with a small `PortalAdapter` sub-strategy per portal so adding one is ~60 lines.
* **Never bundle multiple ad SDKs into one build.** One provider per export preset.

### 45.3 Placement inventory (implement all of these)

Define `AdPlacement` as an enum + a `.tres` table so the human can retune frequency without touching code.

| ID | Type | Trigger | Frequency cap (default) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `INTER_RUN` | Interstitial | Between runs, on tapping PLAY AGAIN | Not before run #3 of a session; then min `120 s` since last interstitial AND min 2 runs since last | The bread-and-butter placement |
| `MENU_RETURN` | Interstitial | Returning to main menu from a run | min `180 s` gap; never back-to-back with `INTER_RUN` | |
| `REVIVE` | **Rewarded** | Game-over "Revive" button | max `1` per run, `4` per session | Highest-value placement; see `§45.5` |
| `DOUBLE_COINS` | **Rewarded** | Game-over "×2 coins" button | `1` per run | Pure upside, high opt-in |
| `MISSION_REROLL` | **Rewarded** | Missions screen | `1` per day | |
| `SKIN_TRIAL` | **Rewarded** | Skins screen: unlock a locked skin for 24 h | `2` per day | Excellent conversion driver |
| `STARTER_BOOST` | **Rewarded** | Pre-run opt-in: start with Power 24 + Aegis | `1` per session | Optional, test its impact on retention |
| `BANNER_MENU` | Banner | Main menu, skins, missions screens only | always (if enabled) | **Never during gameplay** on mobile |
| `APP_OPEN` | App Open | Cold start, from run #2 onward | `1` per 4 h | Mobile only; skip on web |

**Hard rules:**
* **Zero ads before or during the very first run of a fresh install.** First-session retention beats first-session revenue.
* **No interstitial ever interrupts live gameplay.** Only at natural stops.
* **No banner is ever visible during gameplay on mobile.** On desktop web, an off-canvas side banner is acceptable if the portal provides one.
* Total interstitials capped at `session_interstitial_cap = 6`.
* All caps, gaps, and enables live in `ads.tres` and are overridable by `RemoteConfig` so the human can tune live without a rebuild.

### 45.4 Ad pacing model

Implement `AdPacer` inside `AdManager`:

```
can_show(placement) ==
      ads_enabled
  AND consent_ok_for(placement)
  AND not no_ads_purchased
  AND session_runs >= placement.min_run_index
  AND time_since_last_interstitial >= placement.min_gap_seconds
  AND session_count(placement) < placement.session_cap
  AND day_count(placement) < placement.daily_cap
  AND provider.is_available(placement)
```

Log every decision (`allowed` / `blocked` + reason) to the debug ad panel. This makes tuning empirical instead of superstitious.

### 45.5 Rewarded ads are the product (design them properly)

Rewarded video pays multiples of an interstitial and players *choose* it. Design every reward so it is **desirable but never necessary**:

* **Revive** restores you at `65%` of your power at death, with `2.5 s` of invulnerability and a spawn point away from immediate threats. `1` per run so it never trivialises the leaderboard. A revived run is flagged `revived: true` in the leaderboard entry and in analytics.
* **×2 Coins** doubles the run's coin payout only. No score effect (so leaderboards remain honest).
* **Starter Boost** is opt-in before a run, with a visible 5-second skip timer so it never feels like a wall.
* Every rewarded prompt must state **exactly** what you get and that it involves watching a video, *before* the ad starts. This is both an ethical requirement and a portal/AdMob policy requirement.
* If the ad fails, times out, or the user closes it early: **explain it plainly** ("No ad available right now") and, for `NO_FILL`/`ERROR` (not for user-skip), grant the reward anyway on `grant_on_no_fill = true` for non-competitive rewards (coins, mission reroll). Never grant a *revive* on failure — grant it on `SHOWN_COMPLETED` only, but make the failure message friendly and immediately offer PLAY AGAIN.

### 45.6 The ad-safety contract (this is where most games break)

Before **any** full-screen ad:

1. `GameManager.request_state(PAUSED_FOR_AD)`.
2. `get_tree().paused = true` (all gameplay nodes `PROCESS_MODE_PAUSABLE`; UI and AdManager `PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS`).
3. `AudioManager.duck_all(-80 dB)` — instantly, not faded.
4. `InputManager.set_suspended(true)`.
5. Show `AdOverlay` (a full-screen opaque panel) so the game is never visible behind a partially-transparent ad.
6. Start a **watchdog timer** (`ad_timeout = 12 s` for load, `90 s` for show). If it fires, force-resolve as `TIMEOUT` and restore the game. **The game must never be able to get stuck behind an ad.** This watchdog is mandatory and must be unit-tested.

After the ad resolves (by any path — success, skip, error, timeout, or the browser tab losing focus):

7. Hide overlay → restore input → restore audio → unpause → `GameManager` returns to the previous state → emit `ad_finished`.
8. Also handle `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_FOCUS_OUT/IN` and web `visibilitychange`: if the tab is backgrounded mid-ad, resolve cleanly on return.

Everything in steps 1–8 lives in `AdManager` and is executed identically for every provider. Gameplay code calls exactly one thing:

